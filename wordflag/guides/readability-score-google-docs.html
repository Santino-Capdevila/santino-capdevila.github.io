How to check the readability score of a Google Doc

Google Docs does not show a readability score. Tools > Word count only shows pages, words and characters. You can still work out the most common score, the Flesch-Kincaid grade level, with what Docs gives you.

What the score means

The Flesch-Kincaid grade level estimates the US school grade a reader needs to follow a text. It only looks at two things: how long your sentences are and how long your words are (in syllables). A grade of 8 means a typical 13–14-year-old can read it comfortably. Much web and business writing aims for grade 8 or lower.

The formula:

grade = 0.39 × (words ÷ sentences) + 11.8 × (syllables ÷ words) − 15.59

Work it out in Google Docs

1. Count the words

Open Tools > Word count (Ctrl+Shift+C, or Cmd+Shift+C on a Mac). If you select a paragraph first, it shows the words in your selection and in the whole document.

2. Count the sentences with Find and replace

  1. Open Edit > Find and replace (Ctrl+H, or Cmd+Shift+H on a Mac).
  2. Check Use regular expressions.
  3. In Find, paste: [.!?](\s|$)
  4. Docs shows a count such as "1 of 8": that second number is roughly your number of sentences. Do not click Replace.

It counts each period, question mark or exclamation mark followed by a space or a line end, so abbreviations like "e.g. " add a few extra sentences.

3. Estimate the syllables

Docs cannot count syllables. Take a sample of 100 words from the middle of the text, count the syllables by hand (say each word aloud), and use that sample's syllables per word for the whole text. Most English prose lands between 1.3 and 1.6 syllables per word.

4. Put it together

Example: 600 words, 40 sentences, and a sample of 100 words with 145 syllables:

0.39 × (600 ÷ 40) + 11.8 × 1.45 − 15.59
= 5.85 + 17.11 − 15.59
= grade 7.4

How to lower the grade

  • Split long sentences. Sentence length is half of the formula. Anything over 20 words is worth a second look, and over 30 almost always reads better as two sentences.
  • Prefer short words where they mean the same thing: "use" for "utilize", "help" for "facilitate", "to" for "in order to".
  • Cut filler such as "very", "really" and "basically". See how to highlight every instance of a word.
  • Use the active voice where you can; passive sentences tend to be longer. See how to find passive voice in Google Docs.

A readability score is a rough guide, not a verdict on your writing. Technical terms push the grade up even when the text is clear to its readers.
