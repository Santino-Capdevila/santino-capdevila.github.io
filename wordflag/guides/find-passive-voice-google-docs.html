How to find passive voice in Google Docs

Google Docs does not have a passive voice checker. But its Find and replace box understands patterns (regular expressions), and one pattern finds most passive sentences in seconds.

What passive voice looks like

In the passive voice, the thing acted on comes first and the doer comes later, or not at all:

  • Passive: "The report was written by the team." Active: "The team wrote the report."
  • Passive: "Mistakes were made." (Who made them?)

The pattern is a form of "to be" (is, are, was, were, be, been, being) followed by a past participle (written, approved, made). Passive voice is not wrong, but too much of it makes text longer and vaguer.

Find it with Find and replace

  1. Open Edit > Find and replace (Ctrl+H, or Cmd+Shift+H on a Mac).
  2. Check Use regular expressions.
  3. In Find, paste: 
    \b(am|is|are|was|were|be|been|being)\s+\w+(ed|en)\b
  4. Docs marks every match and shows the count, for example "1 of 4". Click Next to step through them and decide on each one. Do not click Replace.

Want a permanent mark? While a match is selected you can close the box and apply a highlight color, or use the multi-select method in how to highlight all instances of a word.

What this pattern misses (and what it flags by mistake)

  • Irregular participles that do not end in -ed or -en, such as "made", "sent", "built" or "put". "Mistakes were made" is not found. To catch them, add them to the end of the pattern: \b(am|is|are|was|were|be|been|being)\s+(\w+(ed|en)|made|sent|built|put|done|found|kept|held)\b
  • Words in between, as in "was quickly approved".
  • Adjectives that look like participles: "She was tired" or "The room is open" are matched but are not passive. That is why you review each match instead of replacing.

How to fix a passive sentence

Ask "who did it?", put that at the start, and use the active verb:

  • "The budget was approved by the board." → "The board approved the budget."
  • "The emails were sent yesterday." → "We sent the emails yesterday."

Keep the passive when the doer is unknown or does not matter ("The building was built in 1920"), or when you want the focus on the result.
